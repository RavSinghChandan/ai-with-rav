@callout|yellow|In One Line: ROAS counts the money coming in. ROI counts what you keep. Only one of them pays salaries.

@h2|Two numbers, one campaign
**ROAS** = revenue ÷ ad spend. Simple, and always flattering.

**ROI (contribution)** = (revenue × margin − spend) ÷ spend. This is the one the
business actually feels.

@image|images/img05-roas-vs-roi.png|Same 2.41x ROAS. Thin margin keeps almost nothing; fat margin keeps most of it.

@h2|The example that made it obvious
An ecommerce campaign in my app: **2.41x ROAS**. Spend ₹1, get ₹2.41 back.
Looks like a win.

Ecommerce gross margin is about **35%**. So of that ₹2.41, you keep about ₹0.84 —
and you spent ₹1.00 to get it.

@callout|red|Contribution ROI: −0.16x. The campaign lost money while its headline number looked healthy.

@h2|The same ROAS, a different business
Real estate runs near **90%** margin, because the product is a brokerage fee, not
goods you had to buy.

The identical 2.41x ROAS there keeps ₹2.17 against ₹1.00 spent — genuinely
profitable. Same ratio, opposite outcome.

@h2|What to show
Show both, and say which one you are judging on.

@callout|green|A dashboard that shows only ROAS is not wrong. It is just showing the number that is hardest to fail.
